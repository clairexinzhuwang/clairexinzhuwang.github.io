Bayesian Optimization for Dose Finding with Two Agents: Participant Allocation and Final Selection

Advisor: Tanzy Love (University of Rochester).

Dual-agent dose finding must balance efficacy and tolerability. Motivated by MARIPOSA’s obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) results, I developed a constrained knowledge-gradient evaluator to assess how the next cohort could improve the final dose choice. Simulations evaluate participant allocation separately from final selection.

In the primary simulation, constrained knowledge gradient increased final-selection mean efficacy by 0.74 simulation units versus constrained expected improvement, alongside 8.30 percentage points more selection above the mean-toxicity limit (averaged across strata and five probability cutoffs).

Motor Outcomes With Nusinersen Versus Risdiplam in Spinal Muscular Atrophy

Advisors: Abdus S. Wahed (University of Rochester); Divya Jayaraman (Department of Neurology, Columbia University).

I compared recorded motor-function changes under two SMA treatments, combining weights for measured patient differences, assessment timing and switching with a model bounded to the HFMSE scale. At shared assessment profiles within 42 months of treatment start, the adjusted risdiplam–nusinersen difference was +0.95 HFMSE points (95% patient-bootstrap interval: −3.28 to +5.38). Estimates varied across models; relative motor benefit remained unresolved.

Self-Affirmation Intervention for Medication Adherence in Breast Cancer Patients

Advisor: Abdus S. Wahed (University of Rochester).

I prepare data, construct outcomes and model adherence, stress and symptoms for a randomized trial of self-affirmation versus control writing in women prescribed aromatase inhibitors for breast cancer.

A proposed secondary causal analysis would compare symptom outcomes under defined medication-use strategies, asking whether patients feel better while continuing prescribed therapy. The randomized comparison remains primary. This extension requires reliable medication histories, clear time ordering and adequate measurement of factors driving treatment changes.

2023

Regeneron Pharmaceuticals · Global Development

Biostatistics Summer Intern · Zhentao Tong’s group

Developed and evaluated a Bayesian simulation framework for dose finding with continuous biomarker outcomes in an ALS drug-development project.

  • Implemented conjugate Bayesian analyses under a normal model for log-transformed biomarker ratios to baseline, comparing known response variance with joint inference for the response mean and variance.
  • Under the simulated design and decision rule, dose recommendations tended to favor levels corresponding to approximately 30% nominal biomarker reduction.
  • At the response levels examined, treating variance as known gave greater posterior support for the target range and a greater tendency to recommend the current dose than joint inference for the mean and variance. The informative priors examined produced broadly similar recommendation patterns to the noninformative analyses.
  • Identified below-target recommendations in scenarios containing two or more doses with nominal reductions below 20%. This finding motivated evaluating a higher posterior-probability cutoff for recommending a dose and stopping escalation.
