Statistical inference

Thesis advisors: Brent A. Johnson (University of Rochester) and Ollivier Hyrien (Affiliate Professor, University of Washington, Seattle).

Inference from stochastic gradient descent for U-statistics

High-Dimensional Pairwise U-Statistic M-Estimation: Support Recovery and Post-Recovery Simultaneous Inference

Manuscripts in preparation · Public release subject to advisor approval

  • Develop offline inference for the final SGD iterate in fixed-dimensional U-statistic models, without a prespecified log-likelihood. In finite-budget rank regression (BT/n = 1), accounting for data and tuple-sampling variation gave 95.3% marginal coverage versus 79.3% with data variance alone (nominal 95%).
  • Develop support recovery and simultaneous inference for the same population coefficient vector in high-dimensional pairwise U-statistic models. The registered adaptive-cap evaluation achieved 99.99% exact recovery and 95.4% joint recovery, reportability and simultaneous coverage (nominal 95%); the theorem concerns a fixed-cap procedure.

2025–present

Bayesian Optimization for Dose Finding with Two Agents: Participant Allocation and Final Selection

Advisor: Tanzy Love (University of Rochester).

Unpublished manuscript · Public release subject to advisor approval

  • Adapted constrained knowledge gradient for two-agent dose finding, targeting mean efficacy under a mean-toxicity criterion.
  • Developed the computational evaluator, implemented and ran simulations, and drafted the manuscript under Tanzy Love’s supervision.
  • At a posterior cutoff of 0.70 in the OSA-derived full-grid simulation, cKG versus constrained expected improvement increased final-selection efficacy by 0.91 simulation units and above-limit final selection by 9.75 percentage points, averaging two strata and retaining fallback selections. The limit concerns mean toxicity.

2025–present

Motor Outcomes With Nusinersen Versus Risdiplam in Spinal Muscular Atrophy

Advisors: Abdus S. Wahed (University of Rochester); Divya Jayaraman (Department of Neurology, Columbia University).

Statistical analysis · Ongoing collaborative research

  • Compared recorded motor changes within 42 months of treatment initiation using patient overlap, score-recording and switching weights with a bounded longitudinal mean model; retained the first complete on-treatment HFMSE as baseline.
  • Used earlier registry contacts to model score-recording intensity, checked calendar overlap and observation-period assumptions, and evaluated held-out prediction and compatibility with the 0–66 HFMSE range.
  • Estimated a standardized risdiplam–nusinersen mean difference of +0.95 HFMSE points (95% patient-bootstrap interval: −3.28 to +5.38), averaging both drug-label predictions over the same actual assessment profiles. The estimate did not establish relative benefit or equivalence. Manuscript release remains subject to advisor approval.
