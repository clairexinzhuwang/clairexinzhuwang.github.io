Applied experience · Emory University

Emory COVID-19 Health Equity Dashboard

Advisor: Shivani A. Patel (Emory University).

I integrated case, death, hospitalization, vaccination and variant data to support daily public-health reporting. My Python/SQL workflow automated data preparation, quality checks and recurring updates, reducing manual processing by about 20 staff-hours per week.

In Shivani A. Patel’s group, I used propensity-score matching to study associations between COVID-19 and changes in presidential voting patterns from 2016 to 2020. I also mapped geographic patterns and analyzed excess mortality at the state and national levels.

HIV electronic medical record analysis

Minh Ly T. Nguyen’s group · Division of Infectious Diseases

I developed SAS macros, SQL queries and SAS Output Delivery System (ODS) reports to automate analysis and visualization across more than 10 million medical records spanning 2004–2021.

I built an ETL pipeline and mutation-classification workflows, integrating the Stanford HIV Drug Resistance Database scoring system to assess genotype-based drug susceptibility and track within-person resistance patterns over time. I also explored geographic patterns as potential predictors of antiretroviral resistance before genotyping.

SAS macros · SQL · ODS reporting · ETL · Electronic medical records · Longitudinal analysis

Applied modeling · Emory University

Smartphone-image hemoglobin prediction

Advisor: Amita Manatunga (Emory University).

I evaluated smartphone images for noninvasive hemoglobin prediction in preterm infants, using laboratory measurements as the reference. Linear models combined image color, camera metadata and infant characteristics; I compared pooled and region-specific models for fingernail, palm and toenail images.

The pooled model had the lowest mean squared prediction error across the study’s test sets. Combined-test mean absolute error was 1.26 g/dL versus 1.33 g/dL for the best region-specific model. Longitudinal dependence and validation in new infants remain limitations; this comparison does not establish clinical utility.

Linear regression · Feature comparison · Test-set evaluation · Prediction error
