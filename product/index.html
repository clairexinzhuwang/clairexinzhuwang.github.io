Randomized Campaign Analysis

A conventional intention-to-treat analysis of a public 64,000-customer, three-arm randomized email campaign. The case checks treatment balance, estimates visit, conversion, and spend effects, and distinguishes observed revenue lift from the information required for a profit decision.

Decision note

Mens E-Mail produced the largest observed two-week lift and is treated as a monitored-rollout candidate. A profit decision still requires margin, send cost, deliverability, and customer-experience guardrails.

Decision framing

What the experiment supports—and what it does not

Supported by the randomized data

  • Intention-to-treat effects on two-week visits, conversion, and spend
  • Joint multiplicity control for six email-versus-control tests
  • No adjusted evidence that visit effects differ by the single planned newbie check
  • A monitored-rollout candidate based on observed outcome lift

Still needed for a profit decision

  • Gross margin and campaign delivery cost
  • Deliverability, unsubscribe, and customer-experience guardrails
  • Evidence that the historical population transports to a new product and channel
  • A stable customer identifier for customer-level duplicate assessment
