Public demonstration · Experimentation

Randomized Campaign Analysis

An intention-to-treat reanalysis of a public, historical 64,000-customer, three-arm randomized email campaign. The case checks treatment balance, estimates visit, conversion, and spend effects, and distinguishes observed revenue lift from the information required for a profit decision.

Decision note

Mens E-Mail had the largest observed two-week lift in this 2008 retail experiment. A new campaign would require fresh validation: margin, send cost, deliverability, and customer-experience guardrails determine whether an apparent revenue gain is useful.

Decision framing

From measured lift to a business decision

Supported by the randomized data

  • Intention-to-treat effects on two-week visits, conversion, and spend
  • Joint multiplicity control for six email-versus-control tests
  • One planned subgroup check: two visit-effect interactions by newbie status, with Holm correction
  • A candidate treatment for fresh validation; no realized rollout or profit gain is claimed

Still needed for a profit decision

  • Gross margin and campaign delivery cost
  • Deliverability, unsubscribe, and customer-experience guardrails
  • Evidence that the historical population transports to a new product and channel
  • A stable customer identifier for customer-level duplicate assessment

Smartphone-image hemoglobin prediction

Developed a hemoglobin prediction model using 1,002 photographs from 58 preterm infants across three NICUs. The research compared image-acquisition routes, including palm imaging, for a noninvasive measurement problem.

The photographs are repeated measurements from 58 infants, not 1,002 independent patients. This was a research study; the portfolio makes no claim of prospective clinical validation.
