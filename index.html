Research interests

  • Scalable learning and inference: computationally efficient methods for high-dimensional and pairwise data, with valid uncertainty quantification.
  • Experimentation and sequential decisions: learning and optimization when evaluations are costly and safety or feasibility constraints matter.
  • Causal evidence from real-world data: study design and analysis for observational and clinical data with confounding, limited overlap, and missing follow-up.

Selected research

Current collaborations are summarized only at the level appropriate for public sharing.

Scalable Inference for Pairwise Learning

Ongoing research · public summary

I develop computationally efficient procedures for high-dimensional estimation, feature selection, and uncertainty quantification without exhaustively processing every possible pair.

Methods: Pairwise learning, stochastic gradient methods, high-dimensional inference, Python

Safety-Constrained Sequential Optimization

Ongoing research · public summary

I study sequential optimization under noisy, multi-objective feedback and limited evaluation budgets, combining theoretical analysis, algorithm design, experimental evaluation, and robustness auditing.

Methods: Bayesian optimization, sequential decisions, uncertainty quantification, multi-objective optimization

JSM talk program ↗

Bayesian Optimization for Clinical Decisions

Public poster

I develop and evaluate Bayesian optimization strategies that balance efficacy, toxicity, and limited cohort budgets, studying when a more farsighted rule improves the final recommendation—and when it does not.

Methods: Clinical trials, Bayesian modeling, experimental design, simulation

Conference poster ↗

Comparative Effectiveness from Longitudinal Registry Data

Applied collaboration · public summary

I am developing a target-trial-style analysis of treatment initiation strategies using longitudinal registry data, with emphasis on cohort construction, outcome harmonization, overlap weighting, and uncertainty-aware interpretation.

Methods: Causal inference, real-world evidence, longitudinal data, target-trial emulation
