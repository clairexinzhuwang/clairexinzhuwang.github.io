Reliable Inference for Ranking at Scale

Ongoing research · public summary only

Question: Can a pairwise model remain reliable without enumerating every possible comparison?

I develop computationally efficient methods for high-dimensional pairwise objectives, with uncertainty quantification designed to remain reliable when only a subset of comparisons can be processed. The practical question is when additional computation is still worth its cost.

Methods: Pairwise learning, stochastic optimization, high-dimensional inference, Python

Learning Safely Under Unknown Constraints

Ongoing research · public summary only

Question: How should an algorithm explore when every evaluation must satisfy constraints that must also be learned?

I study theoretical and algorithmic limits for sequential optimization under noisy, multi-objective feedback and limited evaluation budgets. The decision problem is to weigh the value of new information against the cost of collecting it safely.

Methods: Bayesian optimization, sequential decisions, uncertainty quantification, multi-objective optimization

Final Recommendations vs. Learning-Time Outcomes

Public simulation study

Question: Can a policy improve the final recommendation while producing worse assignments during learning?

In a public simulation study of adaptive dose-finding, I compare myopic and one-step lookahead policies across final recommendation quality, allocation behavior, and safety. In these simulations, terminal and learning-time outcomes do not always move together, so they should be evaluated separately.

Methods: Bayesian modeling, adaptive design, simulation, decision analysis

Designing Credible Comparisons from Registry Data

Ongoing applied collaboration · public summary only

Question: What should an analyst do when the available data may not support the intended treatment comparison?

I define cohort eligibility, time zero, outcome windows, overlap, and missing-data checks before estimating treatment effects. The goal is to determine what the available data can support and to narrow the claim when necessary.

Methods: Target-trial emulation, causal inference, longitudinal data, overlap diagnostics
