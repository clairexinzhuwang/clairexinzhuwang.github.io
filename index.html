Thesis work

Two manuscripts in preparation on statistical inference. Manuscripts and research code require advisor approval before public release.

Thesis advisors: Brent A. Johnson (University of Rochester) and Ollivier Hyrien (Affiliate Professor, University of Washington, Seattle).

01

Inference from stochastic gradient descent for U-statistics

U-statistic criteria can require combinatorially many kernel evaluations. SGD reduces this cost by sampling kernel terms from a fixed dataset. We develop Wald inference for the population minimizer from the last SGD iterate, without a prespecified likelihood model. Under regularity and stability conditions, we establish a joint normal limit as sample size and computational budget increase, with asymptotically independent data and algorithmic errors. Both covariance components are consistently estimated without evaluating the complete criterion or rerunning the optimizer. The method covers generalized multisample U-statistic criteria. At larger sample sizes, simulations in rank regression, bipartite ranking and metric learning show close agreement between estimated standard errors and empirical standard deviations, with coverage near the nominal level.

02

High-Dimensional Pairwise U-Statistic M-Estimation: Support Recovery and Post-Recovery Simultaneous Inference

I study support recovery and simultaneous inference for the same population coefficients in high-dimensional pairwise U-statistic models. In the registered adaptive-cap simulations, exact recovery was 99.99%; recovery, reportability and simultaneous coverage occurred together in 95.4% of runs (nominal 95%). The theoretical guarantee applies to a fixed cap.

04

Bayesian Optimization for Dose Finding with Two Agents: Participant Allocation and Final Selection

Dual-agent dose finding must balance efficacy and tolerability. Motivated by MARIPOSA’s obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) results, I developed a constrained knowledge-gradient evaluator to assess how the next cohort could improve the final dose choice. Simulations evaluate participant allocation separately from final selection.

In the primary simulation, constrained knowledge gradient increased final-selection mean efficacy by 0.74 simulation units versus constrained expected improvement, alongside 8.30 percentage points more selection above the mean-toxicity limit (averaged across strata and five probability cutoffs).

05

Motor Outcomes With Nusinersen Versus Risdiplam in Spinal Muscular Atrophy

Advisors: Abdus S. Wahed (University of Rochester); Divya Jayaraman (Department of Neurology, Columbia University).

I compared recorded motor-function changes under two SMA treatments, combining weights for measured patient differences, assessment timing and switching with a model bounded to the HFMSE scale. At shared assessment profiles within 42 months of treatment start, the adjusted risdiplam–nusinersen difference was +0.95 HFMSE points (95% patient-bootstrap interval: −3.28 to +5.38). Estimates varied across models; relative motor benefit remained unresolved.

06

Self-Affirmation Intervention for Medication Adherence in Breast Cancer Patients

Advisor: Abdus S. Wahed (University of Rochester).

I prepare data, construct outcomes and model adherence, stress and symptoms for a randomized trial of self-affirmation versus control writing in women prescribed aromatase inhibitors for breast cancer.

A proposed secondary causal analysis would compare symptom outcomes under defined medication-use strategies, asking whether patients feel better while continuing prescribed therapy. The randomized comparison remains primary. This extension requires reliable medication histories, clear time ordering and adequate measurement of factors driving treatment changes.

Background

2023

Biostatistics Summer Intern

Regeneron Pharmaceuticals · Global Development · Zhentao Tong’s group

Developed a simulation framework for Bayesian dose finding with continuous biomarker outcomes in an ALS drug-development project. Under a normal model for log-transformed ratios to baseline, I implemented sequential posterior updates and evaluated dose-selection operating characteristics across prior and variance specifications.

Under the simulated design and decision rule, dose recommendations tended to favor levels corresponding to approximately 30% nominal biomarker reduction. I also identified below-target recommendations in scenarios containing two or more doses with nominal reductions below 20%, motivating evaluation of a higher posterior-probability cutoff.
