Research / High-dimensional pairwise inference

Doctoral research · Research manuscript

Support discovery and simultaneous inference in high dimensions

This project develops a computationally scalable route from variable discovery to uncertainty quantification for high-dimensional U-statistic M-estimation.

Working manuscript: Support Recovery and Post-Recovery Simultaneous Inference for High-Dimensional Pairwise U-Statistic M-Estimators

Overview

This work develops an integrated, full-sample route for identifying variables and reporting simultaneous coefficient intervals in high-dimensional models whose loss is defined over pairs of observations. Instead of enumerating all quadratic-many pairs, it builds a balanced objective from logarithmically many random matchings. A fully corrective hard-thresholding pursuit repeatedly proposes variables using residual gradients, jointly refits supports, and retains a sparse candidate set, allowing variables with weak marginal signals to enter later. Warm-started, preconditioned pairwise stochastic gradient descent then cleans and refits the selected model. Sampled Hessians and Hájek placement values estimate uncertainty, while a dependence-aware Gaussian maximum calibrates simultaneous intervals. The theory gives exact-support recovery and post-recovery oracle inference under fixed-separation, restricted-curvature, and growth conditions, with every selection or computation failure counted as noncoverage. The prespecified study varies sample size, ambient dimension, sparsity, covariance structure, and contamination, with every selection or computation failure retained in the reported rates. Separately labelled sensitivity studies examine sample splitting, class imbalance, signal strength, and support-cap quality.

Method

An end-to-end pairwise pipeline

Balanced pair sampling

Build the discovery objective from logarithmically many random maximal matchings.

Residual-gradient hard thresholding

Propose variables after each joint refit, allowing a weak marginal signal to enter once related variables are fitted.

Pair-SGD cleaning and refitting

Warm-started, preconditioned stochastic optimization fits and cleans the candidate, then refits the selected support with fresh streams.

Sampled Hájek covariance

Estimate dependence-aware uncertainty without enumerating the complete pair population.

Simultaneous intervals

Calibrate a Gaussian maximum on the recovered support; every non-reportable failure is strict noncoverage.

Technical details

The balanced objective uses R_N=ceil(log(p∨e)) random matchings, producing B_N of order N log p balanced pairs. For the difference-form rank and AUC examples, endpoint aggregation computes each full-p residual gradient in O(Np + B_N) work rather than enumerating N² pairs.

The inferential claim is unconditional oracleization after asymptotically exact support recovery. It is not conditional validity after an arbitrary selection event.

Root-N coupling for the stochastic refit adds the growth condition log p = o(N^(1/3)(log N)^(4/3)); support recovery itself does not require this extra condition. For the bipartite-ranking example, the coefficient target belongs to the pairwise-logistic surrogate risk rather than to the AUC functional itself.

Formal evaluation

Recovery and strict simultaneous coverage

The design grid evaluates both selection and uncertainty reporting rather than treating them as separate success criteria.

When the support size is known, recovery is essentially exact and simultaneous coverage remains close to its nominal target across the pooled settings. The externally capped route is deliberately more demanding and reveals where weak separation, class imbalance, and an overly loose cap begin to matter. Numerical and empty-selection failures remain in every denominator rather than being discarded.

Residual iteration and marginally weak signals

Designed stress cases show why residual iteration matters: a one-shot marginal screen can miss variables whose signal appears only after related variables enter the model. Joint refitting followed by new residual-gradient proposals can recover those variables.

Interpretation. This is a deliberately constructed worst case that isolates the failure of marginal screening; it is not presented as typical behavior.

Sensitivity study

Full-sample reuse versus 50/50 splitting

An honest 50/50 split is transparent, but it spends half the sample on selection.

In the evaluated settings, full-sample reuse improved recovery for the externally capped route and produced narrower intervals than sample splitting, while coverage remained near its target. This is a finite-sample comparison within the studied designs, not a universal claim against sample splitting.

Failure analysis

Where does the pipeline break?

Formal-grid weak cells and separately labelled post-freeze diagnostics are reported without pooling them together.

  • Weak separation: selection error places an upper bound on strict post-recovery coverage.
  • Root nonlinearity: under heavy contamination, undercoverage can remain even with exact optimization.
  • Class imbalance: too few positive observations destabilize both fitting and covariance estimation.
  • Support-cap quality: an overly loose external cap makes the harder recovery route less reliable.

Current scope

  • Exact fixed sparsity and fixed signal separation; the capped route requires an externally supplied fixed K, with K=2s in the formal grid
  • Independent base observations and Gaussian covariates in the formal designs; identity cells are theorem-anchored and correlated-design cells are stress panels
  • Root-N inference adds log p = o(N^(1/3)(log N)^(4/3)); the results are pointwise rather than uniform
  • Fixed or independently learned pair representation
  • No guarantee for growing sparsity, local-to-zero signals, extreme imbalance, clustered or temporal comparisons, approximate sparsity, or same-sample representation learning
