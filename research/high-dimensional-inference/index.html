Doctoral research · Manuscript in preparation

High-Dimensional Pairwise U-Statistic M-Estimation: Support Recovery and Post-Recovery Simultaneous Inference

Connecting sparse variable selection with joint uncertainty statements when learning from pairs of observations.

Unpublished work. Public release of the manuscript and research code is subject to advisor approval.

Statistical problem
Support recovery and simultaneous inference for high-dimensional pairwise objectives.
Computational problem
Learning from sampled pairs when evaluating every possible pair is costly.
Applications
Rank regression and bipartite ranking.

Why pairs require care

Ranking and comparison problems often depend on relationships between observations. Pairs that share an observation are statistically dependent contributions, so the number of pairs cannot be treated as the number of independent observations.

The project studies how sparse support recovery and post-recovery simultaneous inference can use the same observations while accounting for pairwise dependence and computation. The theoretical setting requires fixed signal separation and a supplied sparsity bound; it does not provide unrestricted inference after arbitrary variable selection.

My research focus

I connect statistical theory with implementation, simulation design, and empirical checks of support recovery, uncertainty calibration, and computational cost. The goal is to make the assumptions behind a joint uncertainty statement as clear as the learning procedure itself.
