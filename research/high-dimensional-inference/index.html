High-Dimensional Pairwise U-Statistic M-Estimation: Support Recovery and Post-Recovery Simultaneous Inference

Thesis advisors: Brent A. Johnson (University of Rochester) and Ollivier Hyrien (Affiliate Professor, University of Washington, Seattle).

We study support recovery and simultaneous coefficient inference for high-dimensional pairwise U-statistic models. Under the stated conditions, refitting with sampled pairs has the same first-order precision as the complete-pair estimator.

Inference after signal discovery

The proposed application uses available participant history and order flow to model future returns. The aim is to identify nonzero coefficients and estimate their uncertainty after selection.

Both criteria use the same future-return outcome but generally define different population coefficient targets. Recovery and inference remain aligned within each criterion.

The application has not been evaluated on market data. Coefficient intervals do not establish profitability. Serial dependence and shared market shocks fall outside the paper’s independent-observation theory.

Information timing and financial interpretation

Delayed participant identities cannot enter an earlier prediction. Features, outcome horizon and validation periods must be specified without future information. Pairwise comparisons here are between prediction records, not the legs of a pairs trade.

Rank regression need not estimate a conditional mean; a bipartite score is not a calibrated probability. Chronological validation, execution constraints and costs matter for a trading strategy, but do not supply an inferential guarantee for dependent market data. The theorem concerns this procedure’s coefficient family, not every model or trading strategy tried during research.

Related context: Gu, Kelly and Xiu — return prediction; Menon and Williamson — bipartite ranking; Harvey, Liu and Zhu — multiple financial hypotheses.

Simulation results

Across the registered adaptive-cap simulations, exact support recovery was 99.99% and joint success was 95.4% at a nominal 95%.

Joint success means exact recovery, a reportable fit and coverage of every nonzero coefficient; all runs remain in the denominator. The fixed-cap theorem does not cover this adaptive rule.

Individual settings can be harder: primary joint success falls to 90.1% in an individual contaminated ranking setting.
