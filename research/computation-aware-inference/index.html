Inference from stochastic gradient descent for U-statistics

Thesis advisors: Brent A. Johnson (University of Rochester) and Ollivier Hyrien (Affiliate Professor, University of Washington, Seattle).

We develop Wald intervals for the population minimizer of a U-statistic loss from the last SGD iterate. One pass estimates both data-sampling and algorithmic uncertainty, without evaluating the complete criterion or revisiting sampled terms.

Manuscript and research code remain unreleased; public release is subject to advisor approval.

ComputationApplicationsFigure 1 results

One pass for estimation and uncertainty

Sample kernel terms; update coefficients and covariance summaries together. No second pass or optimizer rerun.

Objective, computational cost and conditions

Each minibatch supplies kernel gradients and Hessians. Running derivative estimates and cross-batch products account for shared observations; data and algorithmic covariance estimates form marginal Wald intervals. There is no second pass through sampled terms, no rerun of the optimizer and no evaluation of the complete criterion.

Cost of the online procedure

The observation-indexed summaries require O(np) memory. For the rank-one positive-semidefinite sampled Hessians in the studied examples, a triangular-factor update avoids repeated dense matrix factorizations.

Implementation scope

These bounds concern the streaming implementation with fixed dimension, kernel order and batch size. A dense refactorization at each update adds O(Tp³). The simulation driver pre-generates sampled indices and therefore uses O(np + BT) memory; the largest application runs stream those indices.

The V51 archive does not isolate a timing benchmark for fitting plus one-pass covariance estimation. Recorded campaign times include comparison calculations, so they are not presented as standalone inference runtimes.

Why about n log n draws?

T=⌊nBlog⁡2n⌋T=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{B}\log_2 n\right\rfloorT=⌊Bn​log2​n⌋

For fixed BBB, this schedule gives algorithmic variance O(1/(nlog⁡n))O(1/(n\log n))O(1/(nlogn)), asymptotically smaller than the 1/n1/n1/n data-variance scale. Both remain in the finite-budget intervals. The online procedure then costs O(np2log⁡n)O(np^2\log n)O(np2logn).

Conditions and interpretation

This is a sufficient schedule, not a claim of optimal allocation. Covariance consistency requires regularity, path stability and enough shared-observation products: T2/n1→∞T^2/n_1\to\inftyT2/n1​→∞. Independent samples have positive limiting proportions; dimension and kernel order remain fixed.

A larger batch gives fewer updates at the same draw budget; equal budgets need not give equal accuracy. The continuous-response, shrinking-bandwidth rank-regression specialization does not replace the general covariance estimator used for the tied, fixed-bandwidth insurance application. Counting complete-criterion terms is not a measured runtime speedup.

Applications

Relate log claim amount to rating variables among single-claim policies.

Application details

freMTPL2 insurance data · One sample · order 2

The fit uses 23,571 single-claim policies and 31 rating variables. It models the stated smoothed criterion for log claim amount, conditional on one claim. Coefficients do not describe claim frequency, expected aggregate loss or causal effects. Claim amounts have substantial mass points; the application keeps a fixed bandwidth.

One-pass uncertainty estimation

Data-sampling uncertainty comes from drawing policies from the population. Algorithmic uncertainty comes from randomly sampling comparisons with the observed dataset held fixed. Both covariance components are estimated during the same SGD run, accounting for shared policies across sampled comparisons. Marginal Wald intervals target the population minimizer of the selected smoothed criterion under the paper’s regularity and stability conditions.

Uncertainty at finite sampling budgets

In rank regression, algorithmic variation accounts for 58.8% of estimated total variance at BT/n = 1. Its share falls to 6.6% at BT/n = 20, while marginal coverage of the total-variance intervals ranges from 94.25% to 95.10% across these budgets.

Real-data applications · agreement with full-data Newton

The insurance analysis uses 23,571 policies with exactly one recorded claim and 31 coefficients. The readmission analysis uses 41,984 training patients and 40 ranking-score coefficients. Each setting is a single SGD fit compared with full-data Newton optimization of the same criterion.

At T = n log₂(n)/B, insurance fits with B = 10 or 100 are within 0.82 Newton standard errors of the reference across all coefficients. Every readmission batch size is within 0.26 Newton standard errors.

Theoretical results and scope

Generalized U-statistic M-estimation

Inference targets the population loss minimizer without a prespecified likelihood model. A joint central limit theorem establishes asymptotically independent data-sampling and algorithmic errors. Running Hessian and tuple-gradient covariance estimates, together with cross-batch overlap products, consistently estimate both covariance components without revisiting sampled terms. The reported estimate is the last iterate; the experiments use an online inverse-Hessian gain and reciprocal-time steps.

The result covers one- and multisample criteria with fixed parameter dimension, number of samples, kernel orders and batch size, assuming independent samples with i.i.d. observations within each, regularity and path stability. Projection consistency also requires enough cross-batch overlaps along the joint sample-size and step-budget sequence. Wald intervals are asymptotic and marginal. Shrinking-bandwidth verification is specific to the Gaussian rank design; growing dimension and dependent observations require additional theory.
