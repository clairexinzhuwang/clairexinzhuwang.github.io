Manuscript in preparation

Inference from stochastic gradient descent for U-statistics

Thesis advisors: Brent A. Johnson (University of Rochester) and Ollivier Hyrien (Affiliate Professor, University of Washington, Seattle).

U-statistic criteria can require combinatorially many kernel evaluations. SGD reduces this cost by sampling kernel terms from a fixed dataset. We develop Wald inference for the population minimizer from the last SGD iterate, without a prespecified likelihood model. Under regularity and stability conditions, we establish a joint normal limit as sample size and computational budget increase, with asymptotically independent data and algorithmic errors. Both covariance components are consistently estimated without evaluating the complete criterion or rerunning the optimizer. The method covers generalized multisample U-statistic criteria. At larger sample sizes, simulations in rank regression, bipartite ranking and metric learning show close agreement between estimated standard errors and empirical standard deviations, with coverage near the nominal level.

Manuscript and research code remain unreleased; public release is subject to advisor approval.

Notation, cost and conditions

In\mathcal I_nIn​ contains all index selections; H is symmetric within each sample. A larger batch gives fewer updates at the same draw budget; equal budgets need not give equal accuracy.

The complete procedure costs O ⁣((BT+n)p2+p3)O\!\left((BT+n)p^2+p^3\right)O((BT+n)p2+p3), including the covariance pass through stored tuples. With fixed dimension p and batch size B, the schedule gives O(p2nlog⁡n)O(p^2n\log n)O(p2nlogn). For multiple samples, n is their total size and each sample has a positive limiting proportion.

The variance-rate comparison requires regularity and stability. It does not guarantee finite-sample coverage. Pair counts are not runtime speedups; the updated paper reports no full-data Newton comparison.

Theoretical results and scope

Generalized U-statistic M-estimation

A joint central limit theorem establishes asymptotically independent data-sampling and algorithmic errors. Online Hessian and gradient-covariance estimates, together with observation-level projections from stored tuples, consistently estimate both covariance components.

The result covers one- and multisample criteria with fixed parameter dimension, number of samples, kernel orders and batch size, assuming independent samples with i.i.d. observations within each, regularity and path stability. Wald intervals are asymptotic and marginal. Shrinking-bandwidth verification is specific to the Gaussian rank design.
