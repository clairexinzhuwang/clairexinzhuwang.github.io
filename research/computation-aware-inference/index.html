Research / Computation-aware inference

Doctoral research · Manuscript in preparation

Inference after stochastic optimization

How should uncertainty be reported when the estimator of interest is produced by a finite randomized training procedure?

Focus
Uncertainty for the model returned after a finite randomized training run.
Central gap
Classical inference often describes an exact solution, while practice may deliver an approximate and path-dependent fit.
Decision relevance
Recognizing when a compute or latency constraint can change the reliability of a reported conclusion.

The problem

Classical uncertainty calculations commonly analyze a model as though fitting reached an exact mathematical solution. Modern workflows often use randomized iterative algorithms and stop when a time, cost, or latency budget is reached. The resulting model can therefore depend on the training procedure as well as on the observed sample.

That creates a practical inferential gap: a confidence statement may be mathematically correct for an ideal optimizer but poorly aligned with the model that is actually returned. This project studies how to close that gap without treating unlimited computation as free.

The technical difficulty is to characterize when incomplete optimization matters across different resource budgets without simply assuming that its effect disappears.

Research focus

  • Inferential target. Formulate uncertainty for the estimator produced by the implemented training procedure.
  • Computation and precision. Study when limited randomized computation is negligible and when it can materially affect statistical reporting.
  • Validation. Evaluate calibration, numerical stability, and reproducibility across controlled computational regimes and applied analyses.

Why it matters in practice

Data-science and quantitative workflows frequently retrain models, operate under latency constraints, or use randomized optimization at scale. This work studies whether a reported conclusion is robust to the computation used to produce it—not merely whether a predictive score has stabilized.

My work

From theory to empirical audit

This project demonstrates my ability to translate randomized training behavior into a statistically testable question, study computation–precision tradeoffs, and build reproducible validation workflows. My contribution spans theoretical analysis, methodology, implementation, simulation design, applied analysis, and manuscript development.
