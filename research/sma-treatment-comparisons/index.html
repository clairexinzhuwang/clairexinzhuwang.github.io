Clinical research / SMA treatment comparisons

Motor outcomes during nusinersen and risdiplam treatment in spinal muscular atrophy: an APNCR registry study

Advisors: Abdus S. Wahed (University of Rochester); Divya Jayaraman (Department of Neurology, Columbia University).

I compared motor-function outcomes during nusinersen and risdiplam treatment using the APNCR registry.

HFMSEGross motor function · 0–66 points
Hammersmith Functional Motor Scale–Expanded
RULMArm and hand function · 0–37 points
Revised Upper Limb Module

Higher scores indicate better function on both scales.

Treatment differences

HFMSE estimates were positive across specifications; adjusted RULM differences were close to zero. All intervals included zero. Only 13 risdiplam patients contributed HFMSE follow-up and 12 contributed RULM follow-up, limiting precision; these results establish neither superiority nor equivalence.

Analysis

Overlap weighting emphasizes patients whose baseline characteristics are compatible with either treatment. Four patient random-intercept models compare treatment-only and fully adjusted specifications, each weighted and unweighted.

Each weighted fit applies one weight to a patient’s marginal log likelihood; final estimates average equally across the baseline cohort. Intervals use 5,000 whole-patient resamples, re-estimating treatment probabilities for weighted models.

What the baseline changes

Baseline was recorded during treatment. Adjusted estimates therefore compare subsequent function given baseline status and treatment history, rather than total benefit from starting treatment.

Model and follow-up details

The reference models have treatment as their only fixed predictor. Fully adjusted models add time since baseline, treatment × time, baseline outcome, age, sex, SMA type, SMN2 category, treatment-start year and treatment duration before baseline. Every model has a patient random intercept; none has a random time slope.

Each outcome uses its own eligible cohort: 151 HFMSE baseline patients and 150 RULM baseline patients. Within each outcome, the four models use the same eligible postbaseline scores and the same full baseline reference cohort, including patients without observed follow-up.

Fitted differences are averaged over each patient’s baseline-to-endpoint window, then equally across baseline patients. Windows end at 42 months from treatment initiation or earlier at switching, death or the data cutoff. The results are not differences specifically at month 42 or changes from baseline.

All eight outcome–model combinations returned 5,000 bootstrap fits. Valid zero random-intercept variance fits were retained: 15 HFMSE and 29 RULM draws in the fully adjusted weighted models. No numerical failures occurred.

Causal interpretation and assumptions

Overlap weighting addresses measured treatment-group differences. Unmeasured severity or access to care may still confound the comparison. Standardization to baseline patients with limited treatment overlap or no observed follow-up relies more heavily on the fitted model.

Follow-up scores enter fitting under conditional missing at random; treatment weights do not correct selective follow-up. A causal interpretation also requires well-defined strategies, consistency, exchangeability and positivity. Excluding post-switch outcomes means this is not a formal intention-to-treat analysis.
