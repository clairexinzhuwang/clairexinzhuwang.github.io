Exploratory SMA registry analysis

Motor Outcomes With Nusinersen Versus Risdiplam in Spinal Muscular Atrophy

Advisors: Abdus S. Wahed (University of Rochester); Divya Jayaraman (Department of Neurology, Columbia University).

I compared motor-function changes during nusinersen and risdiplam treatment, accounting for differences in prior treatment duration and incomplete assessments. The analysis combines patient, recording and switching weights with a longitudinal model bounded to the HFMSE scale. Both drug labels are evaluated at the same recorded assessment profiles.

Selected manuscript findings · Figure 5 is reproduced from the updated manuscript. The manuscript and underlying patient data remain unreleased; public release is subject to advisor approval.

Clinical context and motor-function outcome

Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) damages the nerve cells controlling movement, causing muscle weakness. Its most common form involves insufficient survival motor neuron (SMN) protein. Both treatments modify SMN2 RNA processing to increase full-length SMN protein. NINDS overview ↗

Endpoint: change in HFMSE

The Hammersmith Functional Motor Scale Expanded (HFMSE) measures gross motor ability from 0 to 66; higher is better. In SMA types II and III, we studied change from each patient’s first complete on-treatment assessment. HFMSE development study ↗

Improvement criterion: a gain of at least 3 HFMSE points, following CHERISH. Its clinical meaning varies across functional groups; it is not an equivalence margin between treatments.

Why a 42-month limit?

The most populated six-month duration interval among all 118 recorded risdiplam starters was >36–42 months. This population included prior nusinersen recipients and people without a complete HFMSE baseline. The rule used follow-up durations, not treatment-effect estimates.

The observed duration distribution selected an operational limit. It does not imply continuous observation for 42 months or define a biological threshold.

Patient characteristics can confound the comparison.

Age, prior disease severity and treatment history can influence both which drug a patient receives and later motor function. A raw difference can therefore reflect prognosis as well as treatment.

Analysis specification and interpretation

Target: the mean difference between predictions under the two drug labels at the same actual assessment profiles. Each of the 119 contributors’ overlap weights is divided among their eligible visits and normalized across contributors. Holding the original baseline fixed makes the difference in predicted score levels equal to the difference in predicted changes. No unobserved, post-switch or post-death assessment time is inserted.

Estimation: patient overlap weights are multiplied by inverse score-recording intensity and inverse probability of no recorded switch. A fractional-logit mean model keeps fitted mean scores within 0–66. Each of 5,000 treatment-stratified patient bootstrap samples reapplied eligibility and refitted the weight models, score model and assessment reference. Intervals condition on the chosen follow-up rule and model; they exclude post-hoc model-selection uncertainty.

Eligibility: the first recorded study-drug period, SMA type II or III, an in-window complete HFMSE baseline, usable age and sex, and a known SMN2 category represented under both drugs. Later complete scores must be after baseline, on or before treatment initiation plus 42 calendar months, and strictly before any recorded switch or death. The recording period also ends at the working detailed-data cutoff of February 6, 2025, which is not a verified registry closing date. The first recorded period is not verified lifetime first-line or uninterrupted therapy.

Interpretation: causal reasoning makes the comparison target and potential sources of bias explicit. The revised comparison concerns recorded follow-up profiles, not the effect of starting, switching or continuously remaining on a drug. Recording and switching weights rely on adequate measured histories and observation assumptions. Earlier omitted-factor bounds and missing-outcome calculations are not validation of this revised model.

What the model labels mean
1. Linear change · Equation (2b)
Models change from the on-treatment baseline as elapsed time multiplied by a trend depending on drug group, baseline HFMSE, age and time already treated at baseline. Uses the earlier score-recording weights.
2. Same linear equation · Equation (2b)
Keeps row 1’s outcome equation and adds time since any earlier detailed registry visit to the recording-rate model, including visits without HFMSE. This comparison isolates the change in recording history.
3. Follow-up mean within 0–66 · Equation (2a)
Uses row 2’s updated weights and a fractional-logit model for follow-up mean HFMSE. It also changes the intercept and covariate placement and adds treatment-start year and SMA type. The difference from row 2 therefore reflects more than the score bounds.
4. Time and treatment-by-time only · Equation (2c)
Models change using elapsed time and its interaction with drug group, with the earlier weights and without the additional clinical predictors.

Notation. i indexes patients and j assessments. Dij is change from baseline; t is years since baseline; A or a equals 1 for risdiplam and 0 for nusinersen. X contains baseline HFMSE, age and log(1 + prebaseline treatment days); Z adds treatment-start year and an SMA type III indicator. mi(a, t) is the fitted mean score, expit(u) = 1/(1 + e−u), and ε is the residual in the linear models.

Coefficients are re-estimated in each row. All models use the same assessment reference and separately fitted patient-bootstrap intervals. These are related analyses of the same registry, not independent replications. The bounded mean accepts scores of 0 and 66 without assuming 66 independent binary trials.

Relative motor benefit remains unresolved. The revised comparison and all three linear references have intervals spanning zero. The point estimates depend on model choice; they establish neither superiority nor equivalence.

Other findings and limits

Both groups included patients with recorded motor gains. Near treatment month 12, mean changes from the on-treatment baseline were +2.5 HFMSE points with nusinersen and +3.0 with risdiplam. Ten of 34 and three of eight assessed patients, respectively, gained at least three points. Different patients contributed at later windows, so these observed fractions are not adjusted treatment-response comparisons.

Assessment history added information. Earlier registry visits without HFMSE improved held-out prediction of score recording. Of 40 patients without a later complete score, 28 had other registry contact; a missing motor assessment therefore did not necessarily mean loss of contact.

The bounded model had higher prediction error. The bounded model eliminated out-of-range fitted means, compared with 18 in the earlier full linear model. Its patient-held-out root mean squared prediction error was 6.08 versus 5.62 HFMSE points, so range compatibility did not imply better prediction of observed scores.

Some assessment years had no risdiplam observations. About 21.2% of the assessment-reference weight fell in calendar years without any risdiplam follow-up score. Calendar adjustment cannot supply contemporaneous observations that were never recorded.

Assumed recording time affected the comparison. Limiting modeled recording opportunity to at most six or twelve months after the last detailed contact gave differences of +0.48 and +0.71 HFMSE points. These are point-only sensitivity checks, without new confidence intervals or verified withdrawal dates.

This selected registry extract supports a conditional comparison at recorded assessment profiles, not an effect of starting, switching or continuously remaining on a drug. Weighting depends on measured histories and observation assumptions. Bootstrap intervals condition on the chosen model and follow-up rule and do not include post-hoc model-selection uncertainty.
