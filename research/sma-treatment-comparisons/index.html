Clinical research / SMA treatment comparisons

Motor outcomes during nusinersen and risdiplam treatment in spinal muscular atrophy: an APNCR registry study

Advisors: Abdus S. Wahed (University of Rochester); Divya Jayaraman (Department of Neurology, Columbia University).

I designed and implemented a longitudinal comparison of nusinersen and risdiplam in the APNCR registry, integrating propensity-score overlap weighting with mixed-effects modeling of HFMSE and RULM. The analysis addresses on-treatment baselines, irregular assessments and treatment switching through an explicit comparison target.

Methods overview · The manuscript and underlying patient data remain unreleased; their public release is subject to advisor approval.

Methodological approach

Treatment weighting governs estimation; the final contrast is standardized to the full baseline reference cohort.

Define the estimand

Average fitted treatment differences over each patient’s baseline-to-endpoint window, then equally across the outcome-specific baseline cohort. This keeps the comparison target consistent across model specifications and includes eligible patients without observed follow-up in the reference population.

Weight the patient-level likelihood

Apply propensity-score overlap weights to each patient’s marginal log likelihood, accounting for repeated assessments with a random intercept. Treatment-only and fully adjusted models, each weighted and unweighted, use the same eligible records and reference population.

Propagate uncertainty through estimation

Resample whole patients within treatment groups and re-estimate propensity scores in every weighted bootstrap analysis. This preserves within-patient dependence and incorporates uncertainty in the estimated weights, with numerical failures and variance-boundary fits recorded explicitly.

Model specification and follow-up

The fully adjusted linear mixed-effects models include treatment, time since the outcome-specific baseline, treatment × time, baseline outcome, age, sex, SMA type, SMN2 category, treatment-start year and treatment duration before baseline. The reference models include treatment only. Each specification has a patient random intercept.

Patient weights multiply the joint log likelihood after integrating out the random intercept. They govern model fitting; the final average weights baseline patients equally. Standardization outside strong treatment overlap can therefore require model extrapolation.

Outcome-specific baselines and a common treatment-duration cap define the observation windows, ending earlier at switching, death or the data cutoff. Eligible complete postbaseline assessments enter fitting under a conditional missing-at-random assumption; retaining baseline-only patients in the reference does not recover their unobserved outcomes.

Causal structure and interpretation

On-treatment baseline adjustment targets subsequent function conditional on baseline status and treatment history. Causal interpretation requires well-defined treatment strategies, consistency, exchangeability, positivity and appropriate assumptions about outcome observation. Weighting and mixed models do not by themselves establish these conditions.

Patient characteristics can confound the comparison.

Age, prior disease severity and treatment history can influence both which drug a patient receives and later motor function. A raw difference can therefore reflect prognosis as well as treatment.
